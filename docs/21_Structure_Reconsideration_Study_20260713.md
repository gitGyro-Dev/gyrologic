# Structure Reconsideration Study v0

## 1. Purpose

This document returns to **Structure** after the preliminary studies of:

```text
Structure Identity
Slice
Operator Orientation
```

The invariant Core remains unchanged:

```text
Structure
↓
Slice
↓
Stability
```

The primary definition remains:

```text
Structure is the mode in which something can be established.
```

Japanese:

```text
Structureとは、何かが成立し得る様式である。
```

This study does not replace that definition.

Its purpose is to clarify what Structure must be able to support after the distinction among existence, change, Slice, Stability, and Trajectory has become more precise.

---

## 2. Established Distinctions

The following distinctions are now treated as important constraints on any formalization of Structure.

```text
Structure may exist without a particular Slice.
```

```text
Structure existence
≠
readability through Slice
```

```text
change
≠
Slice
```

```text
Structure
≠
Trajectory
```

```text
Operator Orientation
≠
Structure
```

```text
Slice result
≠
Structure itself
```

Therefore, Structure must not be defined merely as:

```text
a Slice result
a current observation
a changing state
a trajectory history
an Operator-relative representation
```

Structure may support all of these without being identical to any one of them.

---

## 3. Structure Before Slice

A Structure may exist even when no particular Slice is currently performed.

This means that Structure cannot depend on one actual readable establishment for its existence.

A preliminary distinction is:

```text
Structure existence
=
the existence of a mode in which establishment is possible
```

```text
Structure readability
=
the way part of that mode becomes readable through a particular Slice
```

Thus:

```text
Structure precedes a particular readability
```

This precedence is logical or theoretical, not necessarily chronological.

Structure is not required to wait in a static condition before Slice.

It may already be changing, interacting, or retaining prior transformation while no particular Slice is applied.

---

## 4. What Structure Must Support

After the Slice and Operator studies, Structure must support at least the following properties.

### 4.1 Multiple Slice Possibilities

A Structure must allow more than one possible Slice.

```text
same Structure
→ different Orientations
→ different possible Slices
```

The possible Slices need not all be explicitly enumerated in advance.

Structure must only retain conditions under which more than one path may be opened.

### 4.2 Non-fixed Establishment

Before a particular Slice, one readable establishment is not already fixed as the only possible result.

```text
Structure
≠
predetermined Slice result
```

This does not require indeterminacy in every physical or mathematical sense.

It requires only that Structure as a Gyro concept is not defined by one already-selected readable establishment.

### 4.3 Relation and Constraint

Structure must support relations and constraints that make some establishments and Slices possible, difficult, incompatible, or unavailable.

However:

```text
Structure
≠
constraints alone
```

and:

```text
Structure
≠
relations alone
```

Relations and constraints are candidate internal aspects of Structure, not complete definitions.

### 4.4 Change Without Loss of Structure

A Structure may change internally while remaining the same Structure.

Therefore:

```text
Structure Identity
≠
material immobility
≠
state equality
```

Structure must support continuity through transformation.

### 4.5 Retention Without Becoming Trajectory

Structure may retain:

```text
traces
constraints
relations
continuities
results of prior Stability
```

produced through prior Trajectory.

However:

```text
retained Trajectory effects
≠
Trajectory itself
```

The current Structure may carry history without being identical to the full history.

### 4.6 Openness to Further Establishment

Structure remains open to further Slice and further establishment.

This does not mean unlimited possibility.

It means:

```text
at least one further establishment remains possible
under some admissible condition
```

Whether this is a necessary condition for every Structure, including a terminated or destroyed Structure, remains open.

---

## 5. Structure Is Not a Container

A container metaphor is useful but insufficient.

A container suggests:

```text
inside elements
fixed outside boundary
unchanged vessel
external operation
```

Structure may instead include:

```text
relations
conditions
organization
field-like continuity
process conditions
institutional arrangements
runtime conditions
```

Its Boundary may also become readable only under Slice.

Therefore:

```text
Structure is not a fixed vessel that contains completed establishments.
```

A safer view is:

```text
Structure is the mode under which establishments may become possible,
related, constrained, and readable through Slice.
```

This is a characterization, not a replacement definition.

---

## 6. Structure Is Not a Possibility Set Alone

A set of possible outcomes is also insufficient.

```text
Structure
≠
{all possible Slice results}
```

Reasons include:

- not all possible Slices may be known in advance;
- Slice may open a path not represented as a pre-existing completed option;
- relations and constraints among possibilities matter;
- Structure may change while possibilities change;
- the same apparent result may arise through different paths;
- some potential relations may remain unreadable under current conditions.

A possibility-bearing interpretation remains useful only when possibility is understood structurally rather than as a flat list.

---

## 7. Structure Is Not State Alone

A current state may be one readable section of Structure.

But:

```text
Structure
≠
state snapshot
```

A state snapshot may omit:

```text
admissible Slice conditions
relational organization
retained transformation
continuation possibilities
unreadable Context
```

A state-space model may still be useful if it is enriched with relations, constraints, transition admissibility, memory, and Slice-relative readability.

The mathematical candidate should therefore be judged by what it preserves, not by its field name.

---

## 8. Structure Is Not Trajectory

The distinction must be preserved:

```text
Structure may participate in Trajectory.
Structure may retain traces of Trajectory.
Trajectory may connect successive Structure sections.

but

Structure is not Trajectory.
```

One Structure may support multiple Trajectory readings.

One Trajectory may also pass through a Structure identity break.

Example:

```text
batter
→ baking trajectory
→ cake
```

The preparation Trajectory may continue while the relevant Structure type changes.

Therefore:

```text
Trajectory continuity
≠
Structure Identity continuity
```

---

## 9. Structure and Operator

Operator does not create Structure existence.

Operator Orientation conditions how a Slice is performed.

```text
Structure
→ multiple possible Slice conditions

Operator Orientation
→ distinguishes or conditions one possible Slice
```

The same Structure may support different Orientation-relative readings.

However:

```text
Structure
≠
the collection of Operator views
```

A Structure may exist without a currently identifiable Operator.

Operator affects readability and path opening, not the existence of Structure as such.

---

## 10. Structure and Identity

A Structure may change while preserving identity.

The earlier case study suggests that no single universal material invariant is sufficient.

Candidate identity dimensions include:

```text
relational continuity
organizational continuity
causal or historical continuity
functional continuity
continuity of establishment possibilities
continuity of admissible Slices
institutional or social recognition
```

A preliminary common principle is:

```text
Structure Identity may persist when an establishment-bearing organization
remains connectable across transformation.
```

This is still too broad to serve as a formal definition.

It may require:

```text
common minimal identity principle
+
Structure-type-specific identity criterion
```

---

## 11. Candidate Characterizations

The following are complementary characterizations, not definition replacements.

### Candidate A: Establishability-centered

```text
Structure is the mode that bears conditions
under which one or more establishments may become possible.
```

Strength:

- remains close to the primary definition.

Risk:

- `conditions` may appear too passive or static.

### Candidate B: Slice-capacity-centered

```text
Structure is the mode that admits multiple possible Slices
without being exhausted by any one Slice result.
```

Strength:

- preserves pre-Slice existence and multiple readability.

Risk:

- may define Structure too strongly through Slice.

### Candidate C: Relational-organization-centered

```text
Structure is an establishment-bearing relational organization
that may persist through transformation.
```

Strength:

- supports identity and change.

Risk:

- `organization` may be too object-like or static.

### Candidate D: Combined characterization

```text
Structure is a mode that bears relations and conditions
under which multiple establishments may become possible,
while remaining irreducible to any one Slice-relative result.
```

Strength:

- integrates establishability, multiplicity, relation, and non-reduction.

Risk:

- heavier than the primary definition and may include too much in one sentence.

No candidate is adopted at this stage.

---

## 12. Preliminary Mathematical Requirements

Before choosing a mathematical field, a candidate Structure model should be tested against these requirements.

It should be able to represent:

```text
multiple admissible Slice conditions
non-fixed readable establishments
relations and constraints
internal change
identity across transformation
retained prior effects
partial unreadability
openness to further establishment
multiple granularities
Slice-relative Boundary
```

It should not require:

```text
one global metric
one fixed Boundary
one predetermined result
material immobility
complete observability
one universal time scale
one conscious Operator
```

## 13. Preliminary Formal Candidate

The earlier candidate was:

```text
𝒮 = (X, R, C, A)
```

where provisionally:

- `X` = carrier of elements, states, or local configurations;
- `R` = family of relations;
- `C` = family of constraints or conditions;
- `A` = family of admissible establishments or transformations.

After the Slice and Orientation studies, this may be insufficient because it does not explicitly distinguish:

```text
admissible changes
admissible Slices
possible establishments
readable results
```

A richer test candidate may be:

```text
𝒮 = (X, R, C, Λ, E, H)
```

where:

- `X` = carrier or current configuration domain;
- `R` = relations;
- `C` = constraints and conditions;
- `Λ` = admissible Slice-condition family;
- `E` = possible establishment family;
- `H` = retained transformation or continuity information.

This is not a definition.

It is only a checklist encoded as a tuple.

The model must not imply that all Slices or establishments are complete and pre-enumerated.

## 14. Failure Cases

The theory is distorted if Structure is reduced to:

### 14.1 A Static Set

```text
Structure = elements only
```

This loses relation, constraint, identity, and openness.

### 14.2 A Current State

```text
Structure = one state snapshot
```

This loses retained transformation and future Slice possibility.

### 14.3 A Trajectory

```text
Structure = complete history
```

This removes the distinction between current establishment conditions and temporal connection.

### 14.4 A Collection of Representations

```text
Structure = all Slice results
```

This makes Structure depend on readability and excludes unsliced existence.

### 14.5 Operator-relative Reality

```text
Structure exists only when selected by Operator
```

This contradicts the current distinction between Structure existence and Slice-relative readability.

### 14.6 Unlimited Possibility

```text
Structure = anything may happen
```

This removes constraints and makes establishability theoretically empty.

### 14.7 Fixed Identity

```text
same Structure = no change
```

This contradicts continuity without immobility.

---

## 15. Current Working Position

The present working position is:

```text
Structure is the mode in which something can be established.

It may exist without a particular Slice.

It may change without being identical to change.

It may retain traces of Trajectory without being Trajectory.

It admits or supports multiple possible Slice conditions
without being exhausted by any one Slice result.

It bears relations, constraints, and continuity conditions
through which establishments may become possible.
```

This is an integrated interpretation, not a new Core definition.

4. Are relations and constraints intrinsic to Structure or only readable through Slice?
5. Does Structure contain possibilities, or only conditions under which possibilities arise?
6. What distinguishes change within one Structure from transition to another Structure?
7. What is the minimum common identity principle across different Structure types?
8. Can two different mathematical models represent the same Structure without a privileged representation?
9. Is Structure best modeled as one object, a family of compatible models, or a typed class of objects?
10. What must remain invariant when Stability becomes the next Structure condition?
