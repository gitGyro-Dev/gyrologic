# Continuity Readability Study v0

## 1. Purpose

This document examines the question:

```text
What makes locally separated establishments, recognitions,
or Gyro realizations readable as continuous?
```

The invariant Core remains unchanged:

```text
Structure
↓
Slice
↓
Stability
```

The Core captures a local realization.

This study concerns what allows multiple local realizations to be traced as connected across accumulation and transformation.

It does not introduce a new Core element.

---

## 2. Starting Distinction

Identity and continuity readability are related but not identical.

```text
Identity
=
whether changed sections may be treated as the same entity or Structure
```

```text
Continuity readability
=
whether separated local realizations may be connected and followed
as belonging to one intelligible course
```

Therefore:

```text
same identity
is not always required for continuity readability
```

and:

```text
identity persistence
is not sufficient by itself to explain how continuity becomes readable
```

A trajectory may cross a Structure identity break while remaining traceable as one transformation history.

For example:

```text
batter
→ baking
→ cake
```

may be readable as one preparation trajectory even if batter and cake are treated as different Structure types.

---

## 3. The Local Role of the Core

The Core captures a local process:

```text
Structure_n
↓
Slice_n
↓
Stability_n
```

Through this process, a local `not-yet` becomes locally readable or determined.

However, one Core realization does not by itself explain why:

```text
Stability_n
```

and:

```text
Stability_{n+1}
```

should be understood as connected.

The connection may depend on what is retained, transformed, or made available between the two local realizations.

Thus:

```text
Core
=
local realization
```

while:

```text
continuity readability
=
relation across local realizations
```

---

## 4. What Must Exist Between Local Realizations?

A later realization may be read as connected to an earlier realization when some relation remains traceable.

Candidate relations include:

```text
retained distinction
retained readability condition
causal succession
shared constraint
response-to-next-orientation linkage
compatible Difference pattern
Boundary correspondence
functional succession
semantic correspondence
institutional succession
material transfer
transformation relation
```

No single relation is expected to explain all cases.

The common requirement may be weaker:

```text
there is some admissible relation by which one realization
can be connected to another under a later Slice
```

This relation need not establish identity.

It only needs to make the transition or correspondence followable.

---

## 5. Candidate Concept: Traceable Connection

A provisional image is:

```text
traceable connection
=
a relation by which one local realization can be followed into,
from, or through another local realization
```

Japanese image:

```text
辿り得る接続
```

or:

```text
辿り可能な関係
```

This is not yet adopted as terminology.

The important point is:

```text
connection
≠
identity
```

Two realizations may be connected without being the same.

Likewise:

```text
connection
≠
exact similarity
```

A large change may still remain traceable if the transformation relation is readable.

---

## 6. Incorporated Readability as a Continuity Condition

Prior study proposed:

```text
once readability is acquired,
it becomes incorporated into subsequent Structure
```

This incorporated readability may be one major source of continuity readability.

A later Slice does not begin from an isolated state.

It may already contain:

```text
prior distinctions
recognized Difference patterns
usable relations
available Boundaries
continuity criteria
response tendencies
updated Orientation conditions
```

Therefore:

```text
Structure_{n+1}
```

contains conditions inherited from what became readable in:

```text
Stability_n
```

This inheritance does not necessarily preserve the prior content exactly.

It preserves enough of the prior way of reading for a later relation to become traceable.

A provisional relation is:

```text
acquired readability
→ incorporated condition
→ later Slice
→ readable connection
```

---

## 7. Continuity Is Not Merely Temporal Adjacency

Two events occurring one after another are not automatically one continuous course.

```text
temporal adjacency
≠
continuity readability
```

Likewise, events separated by a long interval may still be read as connected.

```text
temporal distance
≠
continuity break
```

Time order may constrain tracing, but the readable connection may depend on other relations such as:

```text
causality
retained effect
shared Orientation
response linkage
identity criterion
semantic inheritance
functional succession
```

Therefore, continuity is not reducible to chronology.

---

## 8. Continuity Is Not Mere Similarity

Two states may look similar without being connected.

```text
similarity
≠
continuity
```

Examples:

```text
two identical copies of a document
two independently started runtime sessions
two users with similar behavior
two unrelated social institutions with similar forms
```

Conversely, connected states may be highly dissimilar.

```text
large Difference
+
traceable transformation
→
possible continuity readability
```

Thus, similarity may provide evidence, but is neither necessary nor sufficient.

---

## 9. Continuity Is Not Identity

Identity asks:

```text
is this still the same Structure or entity?
```

Continuity readability asks:

```text
can this later realization be followed from the earlier realization
through an admissible relation?
```

Possible combinations include:

### 9.1 Identity and Continuity Both Hold

```text
same document across revisions
same runtime across a hot update
same person across ordinary change
```

### 9.2 Continuity Holds but Identity Changes

```text
batter becomes cake
a company becomes a legally distinct successor
a vulnerability event produces a new response regime
```

### 9.3 Similarity Holds but Continuity Does Not

```text
independent copy
restarted session without lineage
unrelated event with the same pattern
```

### 9.4 Identity Is Asserted but Continuity Is Unreadable

An institution or naming rule may assert identity while the actual connecting relations remain lost, inaccessible, or disputed.

Therefore, Identity and continuity readability should remain separate axes.

## 10. Relation to Trajectory

Trajectory has been provisionally understood as:

```text
an axis or trace that becomes readable
when accumulated and folded traces are followed
under a particular Orientation
```

Continuity readability may be what makes such tracing possible.

A provisional distinction is:

```text
retained traces
=
material from prior realizations
```

```text
traceable connections
=
relations among those traces
```

```text
Trajectory
=
a readable tracing through selected connections
```

Therefore:

```text
continuity readability
may be a condition for Trajectory readability
```

but is not identical to Trajectory itself.

Trajectory is the traced configuration.

Continuity readability concerns why the tracing can be made intelligibly.

---

## 11. Relation to Operator Orientation

Continuity does not necessarily appear under every Orientation.

An Operator Orientation may specify:

```text
which relation counts as connection
which gaps are tolerated
which Difference remains compatible
which interval is followed
which identity criterion matters
which traces are ignored
```

Thus:

```text
continuity readability is Slice-relative
```

However, this does not mean that all connections are arbitrarily created by Operator.

A tracing must still be constrained by retained relations and available evidence.

Therefore:

```text
Operator may make a connection readable
without freely inventing the connection
```

## 12. GyroAuth Example

In conventional event-by-event authentication:

```text
event_n
→ valid / invalid
```

Each event may be evaluated mainly as an isolated input.

In GyroAuth, a current event is sliced from a Structure already conditioned by prior readability.

```text
prior behavior becomes readable
↓
readability is incorporated
↓
current event is sliced under altered conditions
↓
connection or discontinuity with prior behavior becomes readable
```

The important question is not only:

```text
is this event similar to prior events?
```

or:

```text
is this the same user?
```

It is also:

```text
can this event be intelligibly traced from the prior authenticated course?
```

This suggests:

```text
valid events
≠
traceable authenticated course
```

A sequence of individually valid events may fail to form a traceable course.

Conversely, an unusual event may remain connected through recovery, Context, and retained behavioral relations.

## 13. Preliminary Formal Image

Let local Gyro realizations be:

```text
g_n = (S_n, Σ_n, Stab_n)
```

A provisional connection relation may be written:

```text
g_n \leadsto_c g_{n+1}
```

where `c` represents Context and relevant tracing conditions.

The intended meaning is:

```text
g_{n+1} is traceably connectable from g_n
under the relevant Context and Slice conditions
```

This relation does not mean:

```text
g_n = g_{n+1}
```

and does not necessarily mean:

```text
Identity(g_n, g_{n+1})
```

A Trajectory reading may then involve a family of traceable connections:

```text
g_0 \leadsto g_1 \leadsto g_2 \leadsto ...
```

However, the final model must also support:

```text
branching
merging
gaps
Re-Slice
Jump
retrospective reinterpretation
```

Therefore, a simple linear relation is only an initial image.

## 14. Failure Cases

### 14.1 Identity Collapse

```text
continuity = same identity
```

This excludes connected transformation across identity change.

### 14.2 Time Collapse

```text
continuity = temporal adjacency
```

This treats all consecutive events as connected.

### 14.3 Similarity Collapse

```text
continuity = state similarity
```

This confuses resemblance with lineage or transformation.

### 14.4 History Collapse

```text
continuity = stored history
```

A history may exist without readable connections among its entries.

### 14.5 Operator Creation Collapse

```text
continuity exists only because Operator names it
```

This ignores retained constraints and relations.

### 14.6 Objective-Line Collapse

```text
there is always one uniquely correct continuous line
```

This ignores Slice-relative tracing and multiple valid continuity criteria.

## 15. Current Working Position

The present working position is:

```text
The Core captures a local realization.

What makes multiple local realizations readable as continuous
is not necessarily Identity.

Continuity readability may arise when a traceable relation remains
between prior and later realizations.

That relation may be supported by incorporated readability,
retained effects, causal or functional succession,
Difference patterns, Boundary correspondence, and Context.

Trajectory becomes readable when such relations are followed
under an Orientation.
```

A concise Japanese image is:

```text
連続していると捉えさせるものは、
「同じであること」ではなく、
前の局所的な成立から次の局所的な成立へ、
何らかの関係を辿れることである。
```

No final term is adopted.
