# Incorporated Readability Study v0

## 1. Purpose

This document examines the phenomenon in which readability acquired through prior Slice and Stability becomes incorporated into later Structure and influences subsequent Slice.

The invariant Core remains unchanged:

```text
Structure
↓
Slice
↓
Stability
```

The starting intuition is:

```text
Once readability is acquired,
it becomes woven into subsequent Structure.
```

Japanese:

```text
可読性が一度獲得されると、
その後のStructureに織り込まれる。
```

This document does not introduce a new Core element.

---

## 2. The Central Phenomenon

A Gyro Process does not necessarily begin from an isolated present state.

A later Slice may already be conditioned by:

```text
prior readable establishments
prior Differences
prior Stability
prior Operator Responses
prior Orientation updates
retained Structure traces
```

Therefore:

```text
later Slice
≠
independent repetition of an earlier Slice
```

The later Structure has already been altered by what previously became readable.

A provisional process image is:

```text
Structure_n
↓
Slice_n
↓
Stability_n
↓
readability retained or incorporated
↓
Structure_{n+1}
↓
Slice_{n+1}
```

The second Slice is performed from a Structure that already includes effects of the first readable establishment.

---

## 3. Why Existing Terms Are Insufficient

### 3.1 History

```text
history
=
what occurred before
```

History may describe prior events, but does not necessarily explain how prior readability changes the present conditions of Slice.

```text
past occurrence
≠
presently incorporated readability
```

### 3.2 Log

```text
log
=
externalized or stored record
```

A log may exist without changing how later Structure is read.

Conversely, readability may be incorporated even when no explicit log is stored.

```text
recorded information
≠
incorporated readability
```

### 3.3 Accumulation

Accumulation explains that something has increased or been layered.

However, simple accumulation does not explain selective influence.

```text
all prior events accumulate
```

is too strong.

Some prior events are ignored, lost, reinterpreted, or become inaccessible.

### 3.4 Habit and Tendency

Habit or tendency usually implies repetition.

But one Stability event may alter subsequent readability without repeated exposure.

```text
incorporation
≠
necessarily repetition
```

### 3.5 Imprinting

Imprinting suggests strong, early, or difficult-to-reverse fixation.

The Gyro phenomenon may be revisable, partial, contextual, or later re-sliced.

```text
incorporation
≠
irreversible fixation
```

### 3.6 Preconception or Bias

A preconception is usually evaluated negatively as distortion.

But incorporated readability may be:

```text
useful
necessary
accurate
inaccurate
adaptive
restrictive
```

Therefore, bias is one possible effect, not the general concept.

## 4. What Is Incorporated?

What is retained is not necessarily the whole prior event or the whole prior Slice.

Candidate retained aspects include:

```text
a distinction that became readable
a relation that became usable
a Difference pattern that became recognizable
a Boundary that became available
a continuity criterion
a relevance weighting
a permissible interpretation
a response tendency
a condition for the next Orientation
```

Thus, the retained element may be less like stored content and more like a changed condition of readability.

A provisional distinction is:

```text
retained content
≠
retained readability condition
```

For example, one may forget the exact event while retaining how similar events are subsequently recognized.

## 5. Local “Not-Yet” Becoming Local “Determined”

Structure has been provisionally interpreted as:

```text
未
not-yet
not globally closed
```

Through Slice and Stability, one local path becomes readable as a continuing establishment.

This may be represented conceptually as:

```text
local not-yet
→
local determination
```

However:

```text
local determination
≠
global closure
```

The local determination may then be retained within later Structure.

```text
Structure_n
contains local not-yet

Slice_n + Stability_n
produce local determination

Structure_{n+1}
contains the retained effect of that determination
while remaining globally not-yet
```

The word `determination` here does not mean immutable truth or final certainty.

It means that one way of reading has become available, usable, or natural within subsequent Structure.

## 6. Readability Changes Later Slice

The central effect is not merely memory of the past.

It is that later Slice begins from altered conditions.

An incorporated readability may influence:

```text
what becomes noticeable
which Difference matters
which Boundary appears natural
which identity criterion is applied
which relations are weighted
which path is opened first
which alternatives are ignored
what counts as familiar or anomalous
```

Therefore:

```text
prior readability
→
changed Structure conditions
→
changed Orientation possibilities
→
changed later Slice
```

This does not mean that prior readability completely determines later Slice.

New Context, Difference, Operator, and Re-Slice may alter or overturn it.

## 7. Recognition Example

Consider the recognition:

```text
Today is hot.
```

The statement may refer to one local condition.

Once recognized, it may affect later Slice:

```text
how outdoor conditions are interpreted
which sensations become relevant
whether heat-related risk is noticed
which clothing or action appears appropriate
```

The exact sentence need not be repeatedly recalled.

What may remain is a changed readability condition.

Likewise, recognition of gender equality may become incorporated into later Structure such that subsequent social relations, statements, and institutions are no longer sliced from the same prior baseline.

This does not imply universal agreement, correctness, or irreversible social completion.

It means that a readable distinction has entered the conditions under which later Slices occur.

---

## 8. Authentication Example

This phenomenon directly supports the GyroAuth approach.

Conventional event-by-event authentication may treat each observation as an isolated item:

```text
event_n
→
valid / invalid
```

GyroAuth instead considers that later authentication occurs within a Structure already shaped by prior readable behavior and Stability.

```text
prior device behavior
prior motion patterns
prior network relations
prior temporal rhythms
prior recovery patterns
prior deviations
```

may become incorporated into the current authentication Structure.

Therefore:

```text
current event meaning
≠
meaning of the event in isolation
```

The current event is sliced relative to retained readability from prior trajectory-related traces.

This supports the distinction:

```text
valid events
≠
stable trajectory
```

A valid event may conflict with incorporated continuity.

An unusual event may still be readable as continuous when prior and surrounding relations support it.

## 9. Relation to Trajectory

Trajectory is not the incorporated readability itself.

```text
incorporated readability
≠
Trajectory
```

Trajectory may become readable by tracing accumulated and folded relations among prior Gyro realizations.

Incorporated readability concerns how prior readability changes the present Structure before that tracing is performed.

A useful distinction is:

```text
Trajectory
=
a readable tracing across retained relations and traces

incorporated readability
=
a retained condition that changes how later Slice becomes possible
```

They are closely related but not identical.

Trajectory may reveal incorporated readability, and incorporated readability may influence which Trajectory becomes readable.

---

## 10. Relation to Operator Orientation

Incorporated readability may influence Operator Orientation.

However:

```text
incorporated readability
≠
Orientation
```

Orientation is the directional condition for a particular Slice.

Incorporated readability is part of the prior condition from which Orientation may be formed, inherited, constrained, or updated.

A provisional relation is:

```text
retained readability
→
Orientation conditions
→
Slice
```

This may occur explicitly or implicitly.

The Operator may be unable to state which prior readability is influencing the current Orientation.

---

## 11. Positive and Negative Effects

Incorporated readability is not inherently beneficial.

It may support:

```text
learning
recognition
continuity
expertise
fast anomaly detection
stable identity reading
```

It may also support:

```text
bias
premature closure
habitual misreading
stereotype
false continuity
failure to detect novelty
```

Therefore, Gyro Logic should not identify retention with correctness.

```text
retained readability
≠
true readability
```

Re-Slice, new Context, Difference, and Operator Response may be required to revise incorporated conditions.

## 12. Candidate Terms

No new canonical term is adopted.

Candidate descriptions include:

```text
incorporated readability
retained readability
sedimented readability
embedded readability
readability inheritance
readability carryover
```

Each has limitations.

### Incorporated Readability

Emphasizes that readability becomes part of later Structure.

Risk: may suggest a clean insertion of a discrete object.

### Retained Readability

Emphasizes persistence.

Risk: may sound like passive storage.

### Sedimented Readability

Emphasizes accumulation and layering.

Risk: may imply immobility or only gradual formation.

### Readability Inheritance

Emphasizes transmission to the next Structure.

Risk: may suggest exact copying.

At this stage, `incorporated readability` is used only as a study label.

## 13. Preliminary Formal Image

Let:

```text
S_n = Structure at a process section
Σ_n = Slice
σ_n = Stability state
ρ_n = readability condition retained from prior realizations
```

A minimal conceptual relation may be:

```text
ρ_{n+1} = U(ρ_n, Σ_n, σ_n, c_n)
```

and:

```text
S_{n+1} = F(S_n, ρ_{n+1})
```

A later Slice may then depend on incorporated readability:

```text
Σ_{n+1} = G(S_{n+1}, B_{n+1}, ρ_{n+1})
```

where `B` is Operator Orientation and `c` is Context.

These expressions are only exploratory.

They do not imply that:

```text
readability is one scalar
all prior readability is retained
Structure is completely represented by a state variable
later Slice is deterministically fixed
```

A relation-based model may ultimately be more appropriate than an update function.

## 14. Failure Cases

### 14.1 Log Collapse

```text
incorporated readability = stored log
```

This ignores implicit changes in how later Structure is read.

### 14.2 History Collapse

```text
incorporated readability = all prior events
```

This ignores selection, loss, reinterpretation, and relevance.

### 14.3 Habit Collapse

```text
incorporated readability = repeated behavior only
```

This excludes one-time recognition and non-behavioral understanding.

### 14.4 Truth Collapse

```text
what is incorporated must be correct
```

This excludes bias, error, and inherited misreading.

### 14.5 Deterministic Collapse

```text
prior readability completely determines later Slice
```

This removes new Context, Operator change, Difference, and Re-Slice.

### 14.6 Trajectory Collapse

```text
incorporated readability = Trajectory
```

This confuses a retained present condition with a later relational tracing.

## 15. Current Working Position

The present working position is:

```text
A Gyro Process does not treat each event as fully isolated.

When a path becomes readable through Slice and Stability,
some aspect of that readability may become incorporated
into later Structure.

The incorporated readability changes the conditions
under which later Orientation and Slice occur.

It is not identical to history, log, habit, imprinting,
preconception, or Trajectory.

It may support both accurate continuity and persistent misreading.
```

Japanese:

```text
Gyroでは、各事象を毎回独立に捉えるのではない。

SliceとStabilityを通じて一度獲得された可読性の一部が、
次のStructureに織り込まれる。

その織り込まれた可読性が、
次のOrientationとSliceの条件を変える。
```

This remains a study-stage interpretation.
