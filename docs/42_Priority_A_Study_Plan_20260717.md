## 3. A-1 Incorporated Readability

### Current intuition

```text
Once readability is acquired,
it becomes incorporated into subsequent Structure.
```

Japanese:

```text
可読性が一度獲得されると、
その後のStructureに織り込まれる。
```

The immediate mathematical question is not:

```text
what kinds of content may be retained?
```

because the retained influence may be heterogeneous and domain-dependent.

The immediate question is:

```text
What mathematical kind of object or relation
best represents incorporated readability?
```

Candidate readings include:

```text
state
family of retained readability conditions
relation
constraint
context-sensitive influence structure
update rule
accessibility condition
partial ordering or preorder
```

No candidate is adopted yet.

### Required distinctions

```text
incorporated readability
≠
history
≠
log
≠
memory content
≠
habit
≠
bias
≠
fixed global priority
```

It may influence later Orientation and Slice without directly deciding them.

```text
influence
≠
truth
≠
decision
≠
Operator Response
```

### First study question

```text
Is incorporated readability primarily:

1. something retained in Structure,
2. a changed relation among readable elements,
3. a changed accessibility condition for later Slice,
4. a context-relative influence organization,
5. or a combination of these?
```

The study should begin by comparing these alternatives through examples rather than selecting notation first.

## 4. A-2 Continuity Readability

### Current intuition

```text
A later local realization is readable as continuous with an earlier one
when there is some admissible relation by which the two can be traced.
```

Japanese:

```text
前後の局所的な成立の間に、
何らかの意味ある関係性があり、
その関係を辿ることができるとき、
それらは連続しているものとして読まれ得る。
```

The exact meaningful relation remains domain-relative.

### Required distinctions

```text
continuity readability
≠
identity
≠
similarity
≠
temporal adjacency
≠
stored history
```

The initial mathematical task is to identify the weakest common structure required for traceability without defining one universal relation.

## 5. A-3 Trajectory

### Current intuition

```text
Gyro realizations accumulate, overlap, and fold.

When retained traces and relations are followed
under a particular Orientation,
a Trajectory becomes readable.
```

Trajectory remains derivative and outside the Core.

### Required distinctions

```text
Trajectory
≠
pre-existing road
≠
simple sequence
≠
history storage
≠
final result
≠
continuity relation itself
```

The mathematical task will follow A-1 and A-2 because Trajectory depends on:

```text
what is retained
+
what relations are traceable
+
how a tracing becomes readable
```

## 6. Method

Each Grade A topic will follow this order:

```text
current intuition
↓
required distinctions
↓
examples
↓
mathematical candidate comparison
↓
minimal notation
↓
failure cases
↓
provisional model
```

The following are avoided:

```text
premature scalarization
premature fixed ordering
premature state-space reduction
premature universal relation
promotion of derivative concepts into the Core
```
