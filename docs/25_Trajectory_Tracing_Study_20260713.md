# Trajectory as Retrospective Tracing v0

## 1. Purpose

This document records a preliminary image of **Trajectory** after the reconsideration of Structure, Slice, and Stability.

Trajectory remains a derivative concept and is not added to the invariant Core.

```text
Structure
↓
Slice
↓
Stability
```

This document does not attempt a final definition.

Its purpose is to preserve the emerging intuition that Trajectory is neither a pre-existing road nor merely a sequence of results.

---

## 2. Initial Image

The current image is:

```text
Trajectory is not simply something that continues.

Events, changes, Slices, and Stability states accumulate,
overlap, and fold upon one another.

When they are later traced under a particular Orientation,
a trajectory becomes readable.
```

Japanese:

```text
Trajectoryとは、単に続いているものではない。

出来事・変化・Slice・Stabilityが積み重なり、
折り重なったものを、あるOrientationから辿ると、
軌跡として読めるものである。
```

This remains an image rather than a formal definition.

---

## 3. Trajectory Is Not a Pre-existing Road

The word `path` or `road` may suggest that Trajectory already exists as one continuous line before events occur.

That interpretation is currently too strong.

```text
Trajectory
≠
a road laid out in advance
```

```text
Trajectory
≠
a route that events merely follow
```

Instead, what later becomes readable as Trajectory may emerge from accumulated and overlapping establishments.

Therefore:

```text
Trajectory is not necessarily prior to the events it connects.
```

---

## 4. Trajectory Is Not Mere Continuation

The word `continuation` may also be too simple.

Continuation suggests:

```text
A → B → C → D
```

as one clean sequence.

However, actual Gyro Processes may include:

```text
multiple Slices
repeated Stability states
Re-Slice
Defer
Jump
partial loss
retained traces
branching
merging
unreadable intervals
```

Therefore, Trajectory may be produced from:

```text
accumulation
layering
overlap
folding
retention
partial connection
```

rather than simple uninterrupted continuation.

A provisional distinction is:

```text
continuation
=
one possible relation inside Trajectory

Trajectory
≠
continuation alone
```

---

## 5. Trajectory Is Not the Accumulation Itself

Accumulated events are not automatically Trajectory.

```text
event accumulation
≠
Trajectory
```

```text
history storage
≠
Trajectory
```

```text
sequence of Stability scores
≠
Trajectory
```

The accumulation provides material or traces from which Trajectory may become readable.

Trajectory appears when relations among those traces are followed or connected under a particular Slice.

Therefore:

```text
accumulated traces
+
relational tracing
→
readable Trajectory
```

This is a conceptual relation, not a formal equation.

---

## 6. The Constellation Analogy

A useful analogy is a constellation.

```text
stars exist
positions and relations exist

but

the constellation line is not physically drawn between the stars
```

An observer traces selected stars and gives the pattern a name.

Likewise:

```text
events, changes, and establishments occur
relations and ordering may remain

but

Trajectory is not necessarily present as one explicit line
```

It becomes readable when selected traces are connected under an Orientation.

However, the analogy has a limit.

Trajectory should not be treated as completely arbitrary invention.

```text
Trajectory
≠
any freely imagined connection
```

The tracing may be constrained by:

```text
temporal order
causal relation
retained Structure traces
identity continuity
Difference
Boundary
Context
Operator Response
Stability
```

Therefore, Trajectory may be interpretive without being unconstrained.

## 7. Result, Process, and Reading

Trajectory is not adequately described as a result.

```text
Trajectory
≠
final result
```

It is also not identical to the processes that produced its traces.

```text
Trajectory
≠
all producing processes
```

A safer provisional position is:

```text
Trajectory is a readable relational configuration
formed by tracing accumulated and folded traces
of prior Gyro realizations.
```

Japanese candidate:

```text
Trajectoryとは、積み重なり折り重なった過去の成立痕跡を、
ある関係のもとで辿ることによって読まれる軌跡である。
```

This is not adopted as a definition.

## 8. Relation to Structure

Structure is not Trajectory.

```text
Structure
=
a mode in which something can be established
```

```text
Trajectory
=
a tracing through accumulated relations and traces
```

Structure may retain effects of prior Trajectory:

```text
constraints
continuities
memory
Difference
Boundary changes
established relations
```

But the current Structure is not identical to the complete traced history.

Likewise, Trajectory does not constitute all of Structure.

---

## 9. Relation to Slice

Trajectory becomes readable through Slice.

A Trajectory-oriented Slice may select or connect:

```text
which events matter
which Stability states correspond
which gaps are tolerated
which Difference remains connected
which identity criterion is used
which interval is traced
```

Therefore:

```text
Trajectory readability is Slice-relative.
```

However, this does not mean that Slice freely creates all relations.

A Slice may trace, select, connect, or fail to read relations that have been retained through Structure and prior processes.

A useful distinction is:

```text
relations and traces may remain
without one currently readable Trajectory
```

---

## 10. Relation to Stability

A Stability state is one local establishment.

Trajectory may trace relations among multiple establishments.

```text
Stability_n
≠
Trajectory
```

```text
{Stability_n}
≠
Trajectory automatically
```

A Stability sequence becomes Trajectory-like only when the relations among its elements are traced.

These relations may include:

```text
retention
transformation
identity continuity
Difference growth or reduction
branching
break
recovery
```

Thus, Stability provides locally readable establishments, while Trajectory concerns how traces of such establishments become relationally readable across accumulation.

---

## 11. Relation to Operator

Operator Orientation influences how a Trajectory is traced.

Different Operators or Orientations may trace different trajectories through the same accumulated material.

Examples include:

```text
biological history
legal history
runtime history
authentication history
social history
material history
```

This does not necessarily mean that all such trajectories are equally valid.

Each tracing may be constrained by different evidence, relations, and continuity criteria.

Therefore:

```text
Trajectory
may be Operator-relative in readability
without being arbitrary in formation.
```

---

## 12. Not-Yet and Trajectory

Structure remains not exhausted by one establishment.

Slice opens one path.

Stability locally settles that path as a continuing establishment.

As these realizations accumulate, traces remain.

```text
Structure_n
↓
Slice_n
↓
Stability_n
```

```text
Structure_{n+1}
↓
Slice_{n+1}
↓
Stability_{n+1}
```

The accumulated realizations do not automatically form one line.

When their relations are traced, a Trajectory becomes readable.

Thus:

```text
Trajectory may be the readable trace of how local establishments
have accumulated without globally closing the remaining not-yet.
```

This is a provisional integrated interpretation.

## 13. Preliminary Formal Image

Let accumulated Gyro realizations be provisionally represented as:

```text
G = {g_i}
```

where each `g_i` may contain a Structure section, Slice, Stability state, and retained relations.

Trajectory should not be identified with the set `G` itself.

A tracing relation may be written provisionally as:

```text
R_T ⊆ G × G
```

and a Trajectory reading as:

```text
T_B = Trace_B(G, R_T)
```

where `B` is Operator Orientation.

The intended meaning is:

```text
under Orientation B,
selected relations among accumulated Gyro realizations
become readable as a trajectory
```

This notation is only illustrative.

It risks assuming that:

```text
realizations are discrete points
relations are binary
one tracing produces one line
all relevant traces are already represented
```

No notation is adopted at this stage.

## 14. Failure Cases

### 14.1 Road Collapse

```text
Trajectory = pre-existing road
```

This makes later establishments merely follow a completed route.

### 14.2 Sequence Collapse

```text
Trajectory = ordered list of states
```

This loses overlap, folding, branching, gaps, and relational interpretation.

### 14.3 History Collapse

```text
Trajectory = all stored history
```

This confuses accumulated material with relational tracing.

### 14.4 Result Collapse

```text
Trajectory = final result
```

This loses the relational form through which prior establishments are traced.

### 14.5 Arbitrary Constellation Collapse

```text
Trajectory = any pattern named by an observer
```

This ignores temporal, causal, structural, and Stability constraints.

### 14.6 Objective-Line Collapse

```text
Trajectory = one uniquely existing line independent of every Slice
```

This ignores that different valid tracings may arise under different Orientations and criteria.

## 15. Current Working Image

The present image is:

```text
Trajectory is not simply what continues.

Gyro realizations accumulate, overlap, and fold upon one another.

Their traces and relations may later be followed under a particular Orientation.

When followed, they become readable as a trajectory.

Trajectory is therefore neither the accumulated material itself,
nor a final result, nor a completely arbitrary line.
```

Japanese:

```text
Trajectoryとは、単に続いているものではない。

成立や変化が積み重なり、折り重なり、痕跡と関係を残す。

それらをあるOrientationから辿ったとき、
軌跡として読めるものがTrajectoryである。

それは積み重なりそのものでも、最終結果でも、
自由に描かれた線でもない。
```

This remains an image-stage interpretation.
