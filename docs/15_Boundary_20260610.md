# Boundary

## 1. Position in Gyro Logic

This document introduces **Boundary** as an auxiliary concept in Gyro Logic.

The core principle of Gyro Logic remains unchanged:

```text
Structure → Slice → Stability
```

Boundary does not replace this core sequence.

Boundary provides a way to describe how distinctions appear, become readable, or become stable through Slice.

---

## 2. Working Definition

```text
Boundary is not a fixed line inherent in Structure.
Boundary is a Slice-relative readable distinction.
```

More precisely:

```text
Boundary is a distinction generated, revealed, or stabilized by Slice,
relative to Operator Orientation and Context.
```

Japanese:

```text
Boundary は、Structure に固定的に存在する線ではない。
Boundary は、Operator Orientation と Context に基づく Slice によって、
生成・顕在化・安定化される区別である。
```

Boundary is not necessarily a physical line.

It may be formal, perceptual, institutional, contextual, operational, or mathematical.

---

## 3. Boundary Is Not a Fixed Line

Boundary should not be understood as a line already fixed inside Structure.

Structure may contain latent differences, gradients, layers, distributions, or potential distinctions.

However, such differences become usable as Boundary only when Slice makes them readable as a distinction.

```text
Structure may contain latent differences.
Boundary appears when Slice makes a difference readable as a distinction.
```

Examples:

```text
Cake layers
National borders
Dotted lines perceived as lines
0.999... and 1
Formal type boundaries
Ownership or contract boundaries
```

---

## 4. Generated, Revealed, and Stabilized Boundaries

Boundary may appear through Slice in at least three ways.

---

### 4.1 Generated Boundary

A Boundary is generated when Slice creates a new distinction.

Examples:

```text
cutting a cake
cutting paper
selecting a time interval
defining a search condition
```

In this case, the Boundary did not exist as an actual division before Slice.

Slice generates a new distinction.

---

### 4.2 Revealed Boundary

A Boundary is revealed when Slice makes a latent difference in Structure readable.

Examples:

```text
layers inside a cake
clusters in data
geographical features
hidden structural relations in a diagram
```

In this case, Slice does not create the difference from nothing.

It makes a latent difference readable as Boundary.

---

### 4.3 Stabilized Boundary

A Boundary is stabilized when an ambiguous, distributed, or contextual difference becomes a usable distinction through rules, perception, convention, institution, or context.

Examples:

```text
national borders
ownership
contracts
administrative categories
dotted lines perceived as lines
0.999... = 1 under mathematical convention
```

In this case, Boundary is not necessarily physical.

It is stabilized by a formal, perceptual, institutional, or contextual Slice.

---

## 5. Boundary and Slice

Boundary and Slice are closely related but not identical.

```text
Slice is the operation or process by which Structure is read, divided, selected, or made processable.

Boundary is the readable distinction that appears, is revealed, or is stabilized through that Slice.
```

Boundary may be anticipated before Slice as an intended Boundary.

However, in Gyro Logic, Boundary becomes readable through Slice.

A tentative phase model:

```text
Pre-Slice:
  Operator may hold an expected or intended Boundary.

Slice-ing:
  Slice acts on Structure under Operator Orientation and Context.

Slice-done:
  Boundary appears, is revealed, or is stabilized as a readable distinction.

Post-Slice:
  Boundary State may be read relative to that Boundary.
```

This does not modify the core sequence:

```text
Structure → Slice → Stability
```

It only clarifies what may appear within or after Slice.

---

## 6. Boundary and Structure

Boundary is not identical to Structure.

```text
Boundary ≠ Structure
```

Structure may contain potential differences.

Boundary is the readable distinction produced, revealed, or stabilized when those differences are sliced.

```text
Structure = the field, arrangement, or condition to be sliced.
Boundary = the Slice-relative distinction that becomes readable.
```

A Boundary may depend on Structure, but it is not simply Structure itself.

---

## 7. Boundary and Operator Orientation

Boundary is relative to Operator Orientation.

Different Operators or different Orientations may produce different Boundaries from the same Structure.

Examples:

```text
0.999... may be read as approaching 1 under a notation-oriented Slice.
0.999... may be read as equal to 1 under a formal mathematical Slice.

A dotted pattern may be read as separate dots under a physical Slice.
The same pattern may be read as a line under a perceptual Slice.

Land may be read as continuous terrain under a geographical Slice.
The same land may be read as separated territories under an institutional Slice.
```

Thus:

```text
Boundary is not merely found.
Boundary is read through Slice under Operator Orientation.
```

---

## 8. Boundary and Context

Boundary is also relative to Context.

The same distinction may be meaningful, irrelevant, unstable, or unreadable depending on Context.

Examples:

```text
A blank space in a form is a slot.
The same blank area in a margin may be irrelevant space.

A national border is meaningful in institutional context.
It may be irrelevant in purely geological context.

A dashed line may function as a road boundary in a map context.
It may function as a visual pattern in another context.
```

Thus:

```text
Boundary is Context-dependent.
```

This does not mean Boundary is arbitrary.

It means that Boundary becomes readable only under specific Slice conditions.

---

## 9. Boundary and Stability

Boundary is not Stability.

```text
Boundary ≠ Stability
```

Boundary is a Slice-relative readable distinction.

Stability is the property by which a Slice result can be assessed for continuity.

A safer formulation is:

```text
Boundary is a Slice-relative distinction whose continuity can be assessed through Stability.
```

Japanese:

```text
Boundary は Slice 相対的な区別であり、
その continuity は Stability によって読まれる。
```

Therefore:

```text
Boundary does not produce Stability.
A Slice result containing Boundary is later read through Stability.
```

---

## 10. Boundary Is Not a Binary Line

Boundary should not be reduced to a simple binary separator.

A Boundary may distinguish:

```text
inside
outside
near-boundary
overlapping
not-yet
blank
unknown
void-like
```

In this sense, Boundary is not merely:

```text
A / not-A
```

It may support a richer set of relational states.

This leads to the concept of Boundary State.

---

## 11. Relation to Boundary State

Boundary and Boundary State must be distinguished.

```text
Boundary = distinction itself
Boundary State = relational state with respect to that distinction
```

Japanese:

```text
Boundary = 区別そのもの
Boundary State = その区別に対する対象の関係状態
```

Boundary State should be defined only after Boundary is defined.

Boundary is the Slice-relative distinction.

Boundary State is how an object is provisionally positioned relative to that distinction.

---

## 12. Non-Core Status

Boundary is an important auxiliary concept.

However, Boundary does not modify the Gyro Logic core.

```text
Core:
Structure → Slice → Stability

Auxiliary:
Boundary
Boundary State
Context
Void
Δ
Operator Response
```

Boundary explains how distinctions become readable through Slice.

It should not be inserted into the core sequence as a mandatory stage.

---

## 13. One-Line Core

```text
Boundary is not a line but a Slice-relative readable distinction.
```

Japanese:

```text
Boundary は線ではなく、Slice によって差異が読める区別として現れたものである。
```
