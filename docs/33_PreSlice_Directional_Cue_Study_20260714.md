# Pre-Slice Directional Cue Study v0

## 1. Purpose

This document examines only the first question raised in the foregrounding study:

```text
What becomes foregrounded before Slice?
```

The invariant Core remains unchanged:

```text
Structure
↓
Slice
↓
Stability
```

This document does not yet address:

```text
what remains in the background
how background retention works
how foregrounding differs mathematically from Slice
```

Those questions are deferred.

---

## 2. Initial Intuition

The current intuition is:

```text
Something from prior Trajectory
implicitly orients the next Slice.
```

However, the following terms are currently too strong:

```text
premise
condition
criterion
judgment factor
rule
```

The relevant phenomenon appears weaker and earlier than judgment.

It may:

```text
suggest a direction
narrow possible ways of seeing
make one relation more noticeable
make one distinction easier to read
make one Slice direction more natural than another
```

Therefore, the current image is:

```text
an implicit directional cue before Slice
```

Japanese image:

```text
Slice前の暗示的な方向づけ
```

This is not adopted terminology.

---

## 3. Examples

### 3.1 Ocean

When slicing an ocean, prior readability may make:

```text
salinity
```

immediately relevant.

The ocean is not reduced to saltiness.

Saltiness does not decide the Slice.

But it may act as a cue that makes certain relations easier to foreground.

```text
salinity
→
chemical / ecological / hydrological Slice direction becomes more available
```

### 3.2 Society

When slicing society, prior recognition of:

```text
gender equality
```

may influence what becomes visible.

For example:

```text
representation
wage difference
institutional rule
language use
accessibility
```

may become easier to notice.

Gender equality is not necessarily the full premise of every social Slice.

It may instead function as an already incorporated readability that hints at one direction of inquiry.

### 3.3 Authentication

In GyroAuth, prior authentication and attack traces may influence how a new event is approached.

```text
prior normal behavior
prior recovery pattern
prior attack pattern
prior device continuity
prior anomaly response
```

These do not independently determine:

```text
authenticated
attack
reauthentication required
```

But they may make some dimensions more salient before the current Slice is completed.

```text
prior attack trace
→
network or movement Difference becomes more strongly foregroundable
```

## 4. Not a Premise

A premise usually suggests that reasoning begins by accepting a proposition.

```text
premise
=
accepted starting proposition
```

The present phenomenon may be weaker.

An incorporated readability may influence Slice even when it is:

```text
not consciously stated
not accepted as true
not linguistically represented
not logically necessary
not explicitly retrieved
```

Therefore:

```text
pre-Slice directional cue
≠
logical premise
```

---

## 5. Not a Condition

A condition often suggests necessity, sufficiency, admissibility, or formal constraint.

```text
condition
=
something that must hold or that restricts allowed outcomes
```

The current phenomenon may only make one direction easier to take.

```text
cue present
≠
Slice must follow that direction
```

```text
cue absent
≠
that Slice direction is impossible
```

Therefore:

```text
pre-Slice directional cue
≠
necessary or sufficient condition
```

---

## 6. Not a Judgment Factor

A judgment factor usually contributes directly to a decision or classification.

The present phenomenon appears prior to judgment.

```text
directional cue
↓
Orientation
↓
Slice
↓
Stability
↓
Operator Response
```

Thus:

```text
cue
≠
judgment
```

and:

```text
cue strength
≠
decision weight
```

A cue may influence what is examined without directly contributing to the final response.

## 7. Relation to Trajectory

The cue may arise from what has become readable across prior Gyro realizations.

```text
prior local realizations
+
traceable relations
+
retained readability
→
pre-Slice directional cue
```

This does not mean that Trajectory itself directs the Slice as an agent.

Rather:

```text
what became readable through prior Trajectory
may be incorporated into current Structure
and may influence current Orientation
```

Therefore:

```text
Trajectory
≠
cue
```

```text
cue
=
one possible retained effect of prior Trajectory readability
```

This remains provisional.

---

## 8. Relation to Incorporated Readability

Let incorporated readability before Slice `n` remain:

```text
Q_n = {q_{n,1}, q_{n,2}, ..., q_{n,m}}
```

Not every `q` must become active in the same way.

A provisional candidate is to distinguish:

```text
incorporated readability element
```

from:

```text
its cue effect under current conditions
```

Let:

```text
Cue(q ; C_n)
```

represent the degree or mode by which `q` suggests a Slice direction under current conditions `C_n`.

This is not yet assumed to be numeric.

Possible cue effects include:

```text
salience
availability
association
expectation
suspicion
attention direction
relation activation
comparison readiness
```

No dimensions are adopted yet.

---

## 9. Relation to Operator Orientation

The current image is not:

```text
cue directly causes Slice
```

A safer relation is:

```text
incorporated readability
↓
pre-Slice directional cue
↓
Operator Orientation is influenced
↓
Slice direction becomes more or less available
```

Operator Orientation may:

```text
follow the cue
resist the cue
combine multiple cues
ignore the cue
reinterpret the cue
suppress the cue
```

Therefore:

```text
cue
≠
Orientation
```

The cue influences Orientation, while Orientation conditions Slice.

---

## 10. Relation to Foregrounding

Foregrounding may occur when a cue effect makes some incorporated readability element more available to the current Orientation and Slice.

A provisional image is:

```text
Q_n
+
current conditions C_n
↓
cue effects
↓
some relations or distinctions become foregroundable
```

The word `foregroundable` may be safer than `foregrounded` at this stage.

It preserves the distinction between:

```text
becoming available for emphasis
```

and:

```text
actually being used in Slice
```

Therefore:

```text
foregroundability
≠
actual Slice selection
```

This distinction requires later study.

The following are only working candidates:

```text
implicit directional cue

readability cue
trajectory-derived cue

```

Japanese candidates:

```text
暗示的方向づけ
```

None is adopted.

`hint` or `cue` is currently closest to the intended weakness.

## 12. Current Working Position

```text
What becomes foregroundable before Slice
is not necessarily a premise, condition, or judgment factor.

It may be an implicit directional cue derived from incorporated readability.

The cue does not determine Slice.

It makes some distinctions, relations, or Slice directions
more available, noticeable, or natural under current conditions.
```

Japanese:

```text
Slice前に前景化し得るものは、
前提・条件・判定要素そのものではない。

それは、これまでに織り込まれた可読性から生じ、
次のSliceの方向を暗示し、
特定の区別や関係を見えやすくする手掛かりである。

ただし、その手掛かりがSliceを決定するわけではない。
```

## 13. Open Questions

1. Is `cue` too cognitive for non-human Structures?
2. Does the cue belong to Structure, Operator, or their relation?
3. Can a cue exist without current Operator awareness?
4. Can conflicting cues coexist?
5. What distinguishes cue strength from influence weight?
6. Does foregrounding occur before Orientation, within Orientation, or between Orientation and Slice?
7. Can the same incorporated readability act as cue in one Context and remain inactive in another?
8. Is `foregroundability` a useful intermediate concept?
9. Can a cue be represented as activation over relations rather than over elements?
10. How should false, obsolete, or attack-induced cues be represented?
